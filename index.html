SDLC Methodologies

I will write a paragraph for each methodology of SDLC. My suggestions are:

  • A) Waterfall Model: The Waterfall Model is a traditional and linear approach to software development, where each phase—such as Requirements, Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, and Maintenance—is completed before moving on to the next. This clear structure makes it suitable for projects with well-defined objectives and stable requirements, though its inflexibility can be a drawback when requirements change mid-project.
  • B) Spiral Model: The Spiral Model is an iterative SDLC approach that focuses on risk management. It cycles through planning, risk analysis, engineering, and evaluation multiple times, refining the project with each iteration. This model is ideal for large and complex projects where early identification and mitigation of risks are critical to success.
  • C) Incremental Model: The Incremental Model breaks the software development process into smaller, manageable parts called increments. Each increment delivers a functional piece of the software, enabling quicker feedback and iterative improvement. This model is especially useful for projects where early delivery of core functionalities is necessary to meet immediate needs.

Agile methodologies are better, and I suggest:

  • A) Scrum - Iterative approach with sprints
  • B) Kanban - Visual workflow management
  • C) Extreme Programming (XP) - Focus on coding and collaboration
